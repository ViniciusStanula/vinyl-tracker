# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Casual vinyl fans in Brazil, primarily on mobile, in discovery and impulse-buying mode. They arrive price-conscious: a deal is the hook, not the brand. Secondary audience: serious collectors on desktop hunting specific albums with attention to price history depth.

Job to be done: find a vinyl record at a good price on Amazon Brazil, fast. Secondarily: discover new artists/genres and learn about records through editorial guides.

## Product Purpose

Garimpa Vinil is a vinyl record price tracker for Amazon Brazil. It monitors 11,000+ titles, surfaces the best current deals, and provides price history to help buyers time their purchases. Success means a user spots a deal they'd have missed and clicks through to buy.

The editorial layer (guides, genre pages, artist spotlights) builds authority and organic search reach, but the tracker is the core product.

## Brand Personality

Connoisseur · Obsessive · Warm

Voice: a knowledgeable friend who digs crates at feiras and knows exactly when to pull the trigger on a pressing. Serious about records, generous with recommendations. Not a retailer; not a streaming service. A fellow collector who built a tool for themselves and opened it up.

Emotional goals: confidence (the price is right), delight (found something great), belonging (part of vinyl culture).

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS/startup**: No purple gradients, hero-metric dashboards, feature grids, or "supercharge your collection" copy.
- **Streaming services (Spotify, Apple Music)**: No green/pink streaming aesthetic, playlist-card layouts, or play-button affordances.
- **E-commerce giants (Amazon, Mercado Livre)**: No blue/orange price-tag utilitarianism, product grid sameness, or "add to cart" visual language.

The design should feel like a well-curated record shop, not a software product and not a storefront.

## Design Principles

1. **The deal is the hero.** Price information is never buried. Every screen that shows a record must communicate its deal status at a glance — badge, color, or both. Hierarchy always puts price before metadata.
2. **Mobile-first, touch-native.** Tap targets, swipe affordances, and scroll rhythm optimized for a phone held in one hand while browsing. Desktop is enhancement, not baseline.
3. **Collector texture, not decoration.** The warm-dark palette, grain overlay, and vinyl metaphors are earned by the subject matter. Every visual choice should feel like it belongs in a record shop, not borrowed from a design trend.
4. **Show, don't explain.** Sparklines beat tooltips. Badge tiers beat copy. Let the data speak; minimize instructional UI.
5. **Editorial earns trust.** The guides and artist pages are not content-marketing filler — they position the site as a knowledgeable authority. Editorial quality must match the tracker quality.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Minimum 4.5:1 contrast for body text, 3:1 for large text and UI components. Keyboard navigation for all interactive elements. Screen reader support for dynamic content updates (price grids, infinite scroll). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for all animations.
